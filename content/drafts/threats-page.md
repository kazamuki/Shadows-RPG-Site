## The people (Mortal threats)

Lead: **[CRB]** "Most of what kills people in NYTE City is other people." Then, condensed:
"They're sorted by what shaped them, not who pays them, because an employer changes and a
formation doesn't."

**Shape for all three families:** ruled rows (`.ruled-list`, as on Who Runs It), each with the
kind's name, one or two lines, then the mono readout.

> **Street**
> The city produces them the way it produces heat: people who learned the block before they
> learned anything else, and found the corner paid when nothing else would. Numbers, local
> knowledge, and very little to lose.
> *The line: arriving on the wrong four blocks*
>
> **Corporate**
> The ones with a budget: training, equipment, and a scope of work somebody approved. Predictable,
> and hard to deter, because reasoning with them means reasoning with the contract behind them.
> *The line: becoming an obstacle*
>
> **Syndicate**
> Old when NYTE City was young. Corporations run on policy. The Syndicate runs on relationships,
> and keeping them holding is the entire business.
> *The line: taking something without acknowledging whose it was*
>
> **Agency**
> A formation, not a job: the Chrome Beret on assignment, the black-ops specialist running
> corporate security, the retired intelligence officer who opened a café. There's always a
> mission, formal or personal.
> *The line: getting between them and the mission*
>
> **Tech**
> A life spent inside engineered systems. Problems are systems, bodies are platforms, and the gap
> between human and machine is a question of configuration.
> *The line: interfering with a build*
>
> **Rogue**
> People who grew up outside the structures that shape everyone else: the Underground, the Alley
> Nations. They want to be left alone, and the city has never once obliged.
> *The line: asking questions on someone else's behalf*
>
> **Occult**
> People who stood close to something old long enough for it to leave marks: true believers,
> ritualists, channels for forces they didn't make. What they produce is real.
> *The line: interrupting the work*

## The old things (Supernatural threats)

Lead, condensed: "Every house has its old powers, and they're rarely what turns up on the street.
What moves in the dark is mostly the lesser end: thralls and the newly turned, ghouls, and
goblins and trolls stranded on the wrong side of a crossing."

> **Vampiric**
> Less a species than an economy: something that feeds on the living, and everyone bound into
> that arrangement, most of them entirely human. Vampires plan in decades.
> *The line: threatening the arrangement*
>
> **Therianthropic**
> Those who carry two natures, and the packs and families built around them. They lose control
> less often than the people around them do, and the ground they hold is marked.
> *The line: crossing the boundary*
>
> **Fae**
> Those who came from somewhere else, and mostly can't get back. A fae keeps their word with
> absolute precision, which is exactly the problem.
> *The line: breaking faith*
>
> **Undead**
> What continues after it should have stopped. A thing without purpose can't be bargained with,
> deterred, or waited out.
> *The line: disturbing the places people stopped going*
>
> **Spirit**
> Presences without bodies. Some were people. Many were the spirit of a crossroads, a workshop, or
> a rifle carried by four generations of one family, and they notice how they're treated.
> *The line: disregard*
>
> **Elemental**
> Force with intent behind it: matter that became someone. They can be reasoned with, which
> surprises people who expected weather.
> *The line: standing between it and what it's decided to do*
>
> **Beast**
> The things that generate their own folklore. Everyone who lives near one has a name for it, and
> a rule about when not to go out.
> *The line: being told, and going anyway*

## The ground (Environmental threats)

Lead, condensed, ending on **[CRB]**: "Some kinds of ground turn up in every Territory and act on
whoever stands there. Only the first has an explanation anyone would accept in writing."

> **Combat Zones**
> Ground the city stopped paying to fix. People live there. Nothing works there: no response, no
> record, no consequence.
> *The line: there isn't one. No one is coming*
>
> **Genius Loci**
> A place that woke up. It talks through weather, wildlife, footing and the reliability of a
> path.
> *The line: offending it, and staying*
>
> **Convergences**
> Where ley lines cross and something pools. It makes everything there more of what it already
> was, which is why it draws company.
> *The line: still being there after dark*
>
> **Thin Places**
> Where the separation between here and elsewhere has worn through. Distances don't resolve, and
> time runs uneven. Passing through is fine.
> *The line: staying, or coming back too often*
>
> **Gates**
> A thin place that finished wearing through, or a hole somebody made and didn't close. A few
> are routes. The rest are just open.
> *The line: standing near one*

*(Each "line" is drawn from the entry's own text: e.g. Syndicate's "taking something without
acknowledging who it belonged to", Spirit's "What escalates it is disregard", Convergences'
"arrive early and leave before dark", Gates' "Standing near one is inadvisable".)*
